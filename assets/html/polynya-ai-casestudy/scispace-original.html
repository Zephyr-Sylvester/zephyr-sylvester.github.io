What are Polynyas?

🌊

Definition

Polynyas are areas of open water or thin ice surrounded by thicker sea ice. In the Amundsen Sea, these features play crucial roles in ocean-atmosphere exchange, ice production, and marine ecosystems.

❄️

Importance

These regions are critical for sea ice production, deep water formation, and provide vital habitat for marine life. They also influence glacier dynamics and regional climate patterns.

Polynya Comparison

✓ Similarities

Formation Mechanisms

Both are latent heat polynyas driven primarily by wind forcing and ocean heat flux.

Seasonal Patterns

Both exhibit strong seasonal cycles with maximum opening in austral spring/summer.

Ice Production

Both are significant sites of sea ice production during winter months.

Ocean Circulation

Both influenced by Circumpolar Deep Water (CDW) intrusions bringing warm water.

Ecological Importance

Both serve as critical habitats for Antarctic marine ecosystems and wildlife.

Climate Sensitivity

Both show sensitivity to atmospheric and oceanic climate variability.

⚡ Key Differences

Characteristic Pine Island Polynya Amundsen Sea Polynya
Location Adjacent to Pine Island Glacier, eastern Amundsen Sea (~75°S, 101°W) Broader western/central Amundsen Sea region (~73°S, 115°W)
Typical Size Smaller, more localized (~10,000-30,000 km²) Larger, more extensive (~50,000-80,000 km²)
Primary Forcing Strong ocean heat flux from glacier cavity circulation Wind-driven ice divergence with moderate ocean heat
Glacier Influence Directly connected to Pine Island Glacier dynamics Influenced by multiple glaciers (Thwaites, Crosson, Dotson)
Persistence More variable, strongly tied to glacier melt cycles More persistent during spring-summer, larger spatial extent
Bathymetry Deep trough allowing CDW access to glacier Complex shelf bathymetry with multiple troughs
Interannual Variability High variability linked to glacier calving events Moderate variability, more consistent seasonal pattern

Research Insights

📊

Year-Round Status

Neither polynya remains completely open year-round. Both experience seasonal closure during winter months with variable ice concentration.

🌡️

Ocean Heat Influence

Circumpolar Deep Water intrusions provide significant heat flux, particularly affecting the Pine Island Polynya and glacier melting.

💨

Wind Forcing

Katabatic winds and synoptic weather systems drive ice divergence and polynya opening, especially for the Amundsen Sea Polynya.

🧊

Ice Production

Both polynyas are major sites of sea ice production, contributing to Antarctic Bottom Water formation and regional ocean circulation.

📈

Recent Trends

Studies show increasing variability and changes in polynya dynamics linked to broader climate change impacts on West Antarctica.

🌊

Glacier Connections

Polynyas facilitate warm water access to glacier ice shelves, accelerating basal melting and contributing to ice sheet mass loss.
